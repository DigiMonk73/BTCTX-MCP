# BitcoinTX 2.0: choosing which coins leave a wallet

The plan for #711, part of #251: the owner's decision 29 (2026-10-25)
builds **specific identification** into 2.0 before the public beta. In
one of your own wallets you may choose which coins leave on a Transfer
out, or on a Spent, Gift, Donation or Lost withdrawal, with the oldest
coins (first in, first out) chosen for you until you change them.
Exchanges stay first in, first out. Nothing chosen means first in, first
out, so no existing figure moves. Planning only: nothing here changes
code. This file is deleted once its last step is done
(`docs/temp/README.md`).

**In plain words:** today every wallet sends its oldest coins first. The
IRS allows that only as the default: you may instead say which coins you
sent, if you decide by the time they leave and your records show it
(Treas. Reg. §1.1012-1(j)(2)). BitcoinTX already keeps each wallet's
coins as separate lots with their dates and costs, so this adds a choice
to a transaction, not a new engine. A transaction that leaves one of
your wallets can name the purchases (or deposits) its coins came from
and how much from each; the engine then takes those coins instead of the
oldest. The choice is saved with the transaction and survives every
recalculation. A later change that would take away coins a transaction
chose is refused, with a message saying which transaction to change
first. Exchanges keep first in, first out, so BitcoinTX matches what
they report on Form 1099-DA. Because nothing is chosen in any ledger
today, every existing figure stays exactly as it is.

**How this was made:** every claim was checked in the code on `origin/2.0`
at 96574a78 (2.0.0-alpha.2), and the rule was read from the regulation's
current text (1.1). It builds on `docs/temp/2.0-accounts.md` (the 2.0
plan: 3.2 to 3.4 for the IRS sources, 7.2 for what every build session
does) and on #706, the Transfer form's preview of the coins that move
(fix slot B, being built), which becomes the picker's first half.

## 1. The rule

### 1.1 The sources, read on 2026-10-25

- Treas. Reg. §1.1012-1(j), the eCFR's text as of 2026-10-01 (the
  versioner API, `title-26`, section 1.1012-1): (j) opening, (j)(1),
  (j)(2), (j)(3), (j)(4), and the examples in (j)(5), Examples 1 to 4.
- Notice 2025-7 and Notice 2026-20, as `docs/temp/2.0-accounts.md` 3.4
  read them on 2026-10-15 (identification of coins at a broker, on your
  own books, through 2026).
- IRS digital-asset FAQ Q81 and Q97 (a network fee paid in coins disposes
  of them), as 2.0-accounts.md 3.1 lists them.

### 1.2 What it says

- **Coins not held by a broker** ((j)(1)): "such as in a single unhosted
  wallet". When you sell, dispose of or **transfer** fewer than all your
  units, their basis and holding period come from a specific
  identification of "the units in the wallet". Without one, units go in
  order "from the earliest date on which units … were acquired", and
  "the date any units were transferred into the taxpayer's wallet is
  disregarded". Transfers are named on purpose: the coins you move to
  another of your wallets are the ones you identify.
- **The identification** ((j)(2)): made "no later than the date and time
  of the sale, disposition, or transfer", when you identify "on its books
  and records the particular units … by reference to any identifier,
  such as purchase date and time or the purchase price for the unit".
  It counts "only if adequate records are maintained … to establish that
  a unit sold, disposed of, or transferred is removed from the wallet".
- **Example 1** ((j)(5)(i)) is this feature: two lots come into an
  unhosted wallet; before paying a merchant, TP "keeps a record that the
  10 units … were from the second lot"; that is a specific
  identification, and the units' basis and holding period are the second
  lot's. **Example 2**: no record at or before the time, so the earliest
  acquired units count as sold, whatever the order they arrived.
- **A method isn't an accounting method** ((j)(4)): "earliest acquired,
  the latest acquired, or the highest basis" are ways of identifying, and
  changing between them isn't a change of accounting method. So one
  transaction may choose and the next take the oldest.
- **At a broker** ((j)(3)): the identification goes **to the broker**
  (a standing order counts). Through 2026, Notice 2025-7 (extended by
  Notice 2026-20) also accepts one made on your own books
  (2.0-accounts.md 3.4); from 2027, only the broker's.
- **From when:** (j) applies "to all acquisitions and dispositions of
  digital assets on or after January 1, 2025" ((j)(6)). Before 2025 the
  IRS's older FAQ already allowed identifying coins with records that
  show which units (answer 40; answer 41 is the first-in, first-out
  default; 2.0-accounts.md 3.1 and 3.7), so a choice in an earlier year
  isn't against any rule.

### 1.3 What it means for BitcoinTX

- **BitcoinTX is the books and records.** A choice saved on a transaction
  is the identification; the lots and disposals it makes are the records
  that show which units left the wallet ((j)(2)'s last sentence).
- **The identifier is the acquisition:** the Buy or Deposit that first
  acquired the coins, which gives their purchase date and cost, the
  identifiers the rule names. Units of one acquisition share their date
  and their cost per BTC, so they are the same units for the tax.
- **"No later than the time" is the user's to keep.** BitcoinTX can't know
  when the user decided. It records when the choice was saved, says the
  rule where coins are chosen, and lists choices saved after the day the
  coins moved (6.6, 6.7). Coin control in wallet software (Sparrow,
  Electrum, a hardware wallet's companion) is a choice made at the time,
  even when it is entered in BitcoinTX later.
- **Exchanges stay first in, first out** (the lead's call on #711): their
  coins are identified to the broker, and BitcoinTX's figures should match
  what the broker reports. The 2026 relief for own-books identification
  at a broker is left out on purpose (3, call (g)).

## 2. Scope and terms

### 2.1 Terms

| Words on screen and in docs | In the code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| choose coins; the coins you chose | `chosen_coins` | a transaction's identification: each acquisition its coins come from, and how much from each |
| the coins from a purchase (or deposit) | `acquired_txn_id` | the Buy or Deposit into a bitcoin account that first acquired them; a Transfer never acquires, it moves |
| what leaves | | a Transfer's `amount` (its BTC fee included); a Withdrawal's `amount` plus its BTC fee |
| oldest first; per-wallet FIFO | (no choice) | today's rule: the wallet's earliest acquired coins first (decision 30's wording, #713) |
| chosen on | `coins_chosen_at` | when the choice was saved in BitcoinTX: the books' own date |

### 2.2 Where a choice may be made

| Transaction | From | To | Choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transfer | a wallet | another wallet, or an exchange's BTC account | yes |
| Transfer | a wallet | the same wallet (a consolidation, #159) | no: only the fee leaves, oldest first |
| Withdrawal (Spent, Gift, Donation, Lost; an old ledger's other purposes, which count as Spent) | a wallet | External | yes |
| Transfer or Withdrawal | an exchange's BTC account | anywhere | no: first in, first out |
| Sell | an exchange's BTC account | its USD account | no (a Sell is always on an exchange) |
| Buy, Deposit, or anything in dollars | | | no: nothing leaves a bitcoin account |

"A wallet" is an account of kind `wallet` (`services/account.py`); a
kind never changes once an account is added, so a choice can't end up on
an exchange by renaming.

### 2.3 Not in scope

- **Exchanges** (call (g)). `docs/ROADMAP.md`'s "Later" item stays for
  them, to reopen when brokers support lot-level identification.
- **Coins by address or UTXO.** A choice names acquisitions, not outputs.
  A wallet's coin control maps to it: the user picks the purchases those
  outputs came from.
- **A standing rule** ("always highest cost first") kept as a rule. The
  picker can fill a choice that way (Q3), but what's saved is always the
  coins themselves, on each transaction, as Example 1 records them. A
  saved rule would change what earlier transactions took whenever an
  earlier one changed.
- **Imports from River and other exchanges.** Their rows never leave a
  wallet (6.4).
- **Rev. Proc. 2024-28's allocation** stays out, as decided for 2.0 (#569,
  decision 20's default).

## 3. The calls this plan makes

Each is a technical or tax call, with its reason; the owner may overrule
any of them on #711. The owner's own questions are in 11.

- **(a) A choice names acquisitions and amounts, never lot rows.** Lots
  are rebuilt by every recalculation, and a lot in a wallet may hold
  pieces of multiple transfers. The acquisition is what the rule calls an
  identifier (purchase date and price), and it is stable: every lot
  remembers the acquisition its coins came from, through every transfer
  (4.2).
- **(b) The network fee comes out of the chosen coins, the oldest of them
  first.** In a wallet, the fee is paid by the coins the transaction
  spends (FAQ Q81, Q97: those coins are disposed of), and coin control
  pays it from the outputs it picks. Taking the fee first and the oldest
  first among the chosen is what the engine already does among all the
  wallet's coins (`_FifoDisposal.take_from`, `_LotMove.take_from`: the
  fee's piece before the amount's), so the rule stays one rule.
- **(c) A choice covers exactly what leaves.** No half choices: the
  chosen BTC add up to what leaves (2.1), else the save is refused and
  says by how much. A choice that falls short can't fill the rest by
  itself, since that would be a rule hiding inside a choice.
- **(d) Any date may carry a choice,** before 2025 too (1.2, "From
  when"). A choice in an earlier tax year goes through the past-year
  question like any edit (6.2).
- **(e) An edit that changes what leaves must send the coins again.**
  Changing a transaction's type, From, To (into the same wallet), amount,
  fee or fee currency while it has a choice, without sending
  `chosen_coins`, is refused (422) with what to send: the coins, or
  `null` for oldest first. Nothing is silently dropped or guessed.
- **(f) A choice sent back unchanged keeps its date.** The form sends
  every field on each edit; `coins_chosen_at` changes only when the coins
  chosen change, so an edit of a note-free field never makes a choice
  look later than it was.
- **(g) Exchanges: first in, first out** (the lead's call on #711, kept).
  Through 2026 the IRS also accepts own-books identification at a broker
  (1.2); BitcoinTX doesn't offer it, so its exchange figures match the
  broker's own order and Form 1099-DA as far as they can
  (`review.sale_may_differ_from_1099da` still lists where they can't).
- **(h) Nothing chosen is first in, first out,** so every existing ledger
  replays to the same figures. The migration writes no choice.
- **(i) A write that takes away coins a later transaction chose is
  refused** (Q2), naming that transaction and what to do. That covers a
  delete of the purchase, an edit of its amount, date, type or account,
  an earlier transfer that no longer brings those coins into the wallet,
  and an earlier change on an exchange that makes a later transfer from
  it bring other coins.
- **(j) A choice is applied to lots that know their acquisition.** Every
  lot made since migration 0101, and every lot after any recalculation,
  has it. A save with chosen coins from a wallet holding a lot made
  before 0101 and not yet recalculated replays the whole ledger first
  (once, after the upgrade).

## 4. The data and the migration

### 4.1 `chosen_coins`, a new table

| Column | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | integer, primary key | |
| `transaction_id` | integer, `transactions.id`, not null, indexed | the Transfer or Withdrawal that chose |
| `acquired_txn_id` | integer, `transactions.id`, not null, indexed | the Buy or Deposit whose coins it chose |
| `btc` | `Numeric(18, 8)`, not null | how much of them, above 0 |

Unique on (`transaction_id`, `acquired_txn_id`): one line per acquisition.
It is what the user entered, like the `transactions` row, and is never
rebuilt: `recalculate_all_transactions` deletes ledger lines, lots and
disposals only (`transaction.py:2162`), and must go on doing so. The
model is `ChosenCoin` in `backend/models/transaction.py`, a relationship
`Transaction.chosen_coins` (cascade `all, delete-orphan`, so a deleted
transaction's own choice goes with it, Delete All included:
`remove_all_transactions` deletes through the ORM, `transaction.py:2881`).
The app doesn't turn on SQLite's foreign keys (`backup._copy_own_data`'s
docstring), so the code checks what a key would (5.5).

### 4.2 `bitcoin_lots.acquired_txn_id`, a new column

Integer, nullable, indexed: the Buy or Deposit that first acquired the
lot's coins. `maybe_create_bitcoin_lot` sets it to the lot's own
`created_txn_id`; `_LotMove.add_destination_lots` copies the source
lot's, as it already copies its `acquired_date` and `inherited`
(`transaction.py:2112`). Derived like the rest of the lot, rebuilt by
every replay. Null only on lots made before 0101 that no replay has
rebuilt yet (call (j)).

### 4.3 `transactions.coins_chosen_at`, a new column

`UTCDateTime`, nullable: when the current choice was saved. Set by the
server when a save stores a choice that differs from the stored one
(call (f)); null when nothing is chosen. A CSV import keeps the file's
value (6.3); nothing else takes one from a request. Added last, as each
migration appends its column (the model's column-order comments).

### 4.4 Migration 0101

`backend/migrations/versions/0101_chosen_coins.py`, `down_revision`
"0100". 2.0's ids are fixed since alpha.1 (#251, decision 3; plan 5.3),
and 0100 is the only 2.0 migration on `2.0` (96574a78); 1.x's head on
`develop` is still 0012. If `develop` brings a 1.x migration before C2
merges, it joins by an Alembic merge revision (decision 3), and 0101's
parent is whatever `2.0`'s head is then.

- `upgrade`: creates `chosen_coins`, adds the two columns (plain
  `ADD COLUMN`, nullable: no table rebuild). Writes no row and
  recalculates nothing: **no figure moves**.
- `downgrade`: drops them. A ledger with choices loses them that way, so
  the way back is a copy, never a downgrade (10).
- `test_models_and_migrations_agree` covers it; B1's upgraded-1.x test
  (`reference_1x.db` through 0100 and 0101) must still give 1.x's
  figures.

### 4.5 What the API takes and gives

- `TransactionCreate` and `TransactionUpdate` take `chosen_coins`: a list
  of `{"acquired_txn_id": <id>, "btc": "<BTC>"}`, or `null`. On an update,
  left out means "keep it" (call (e) aside), `null` or `[]` means oldest
  first.
- `TransactionRead` gives `chosen_coins` (the list, or `null`) and
  `coins_chosen_at`. The list of every transaction loads them in one
  query (`selectinload`), not one per row.
- 422 refusals, checked with the rest of a transaction's input
  (`_validate_transaction`): a choice where 2.2 says no; an id that isn't
  a Buy or Deposit into a bitcoin account; a BTC above 0 with at most 8
  places, each acquisition once, at most 1,000 lines; the total not equal
  to what leaves. The words are in 7.1.

## 5. The engine

### 5.1 Today

- `_open_lots(account_id)` (`transaction.py:1658`): the account's lots with
  BTC left, by `acquired_date`, then id.
- `maybe_dispose_lots_fifo` (1576) takes a Sell's or Withdrawal's outflow
  from those lots through `_FifoDisposal` (1708): in each lot the fee's
  piece first, then the amount's, each with its running share of the
  fee's value or the proceeds (#297).
- `maybe_transfer_bitcoin_lot` (1996) and `_LotMove` (2046): the same for
  a Transfer, the moved pieces becoming lots in the destination with
  their date, cost share and `inherited`.
- `piece_cost` (1696): a piece's cost is its running share of its lot's
  cost (#100), whatever order lots are taken in.
- `apply_create` (84) posts a new transaction's lots, or replays the whole
  ledger when it's dated before the latest; `apply_update` (473) and
  `apply_delete` (1236) always replay (`recalculate_all_transactions`,
  2148, through `_replay`, 2183, and `_post_lots`, 2223).

### 5.2 What changes

- **One list of what a transaction may take**, `_lots_to_take(tx, db)`,
  replaces the two calls of `_open_lots` (1609, 2024): pairs of (lot, BTC
  it may give). Without a choice: every open lot, all its BTC, oldest
  first, exactly as now. With one: the same lots in the same order, only
  those whose `acquired_txn_id` the choice names, each acquisition's lots
  giving at most what was chosen from it, the first made first.
- **Checked before anything is taken:** the lots must hold every
  acquisition's chosen BTC; else a 400 (`NOT_HELD`, 7.1) naming the
  transaction, the acquisition, the wallet and what it held then.
- **`_FifoDisposal` and `_LotMove` take from the list** (a lot's piece
  limited by what the pair gives), fee first and oldest first as today,
  so call (b) needs no new rule. The consolidation's `_fee_leaving_account`
  stays as it is (no choice there).
- **Piece cost, running shares and holding periods are unchanged:** a
  piece costs its running share of its lot, whichever lot goes first; the
  proceeds and the fee's value are shared among the pieces as now; each
  piece's term is its lot's. Form 8949's rows are the disposals, one per
  lot piece, as now: only which lots they come from changes.
- **Lots remember their acquisition** (4.2).
- **A new module, `backend/services/chosen_coins.py`**, keeps
  `transaction.py` from growing: checking a request's choice, storing it
  and its date, the edit rule (call (e)), the lots a choice may take, and
  the refusals' words. It gets its `desktop/BitcoinTX.spec`
  `hiddenimports` entry (AGENTS.md, "Before you change…").
- **The replay reads every choice once** (one query, a dict by
  transaction), so a ledger with no choice pays one empty query:
  `equivalence_check --bench` allows 5% (its `SLOWER_ALLOWED`).

### 5.3 Saves, edits and deletes

| Write | What happens |
|---|---|
| Add with a choice | input checked (4.5); the transaction and its choice saved; lots posted as any add (a whole replay when backdated, or under call (j)) |
| Edit sending `chosen_coins` | the new choice checked against the edited row; `coins_chosen_at` set if it differs (call (f)); the whole ledger replayed |
| Edit not sending it, the row has a choice, what leaves changes | refused, 422 (`SEND_AGAIN`, call (e)) |
| Edit not sending it, nothing that leaves changes (a date, a purpose, a value) | the choice kept; the replay checks the coins are still held on the new date |
| Edit or delete of another row that takes chosen coins away | refused by the replay (`NOT_HELD`), wrapped as today ("Not changed:", "Not deleted:", "Not saved: with it, the account would lack BTC…"): call (i), Q2 |
| Delete of a row with a choice | its choice goes with it |
| Delete All | every choice goes (4.1) |
| Recalculate Ledger, a tax timezone change, a restore | choices are read, never written; a ledger every write kept whole replays whole |

A replay can newly stop only if a later BitcoinTX changes how coins are
taken. Ledger Review already says so before anything moves
(`RECALC_STOPS`, its `recalc_changes` dry run), and the stop names the
choice (7.1).

### 5.4 What stays first in, first out

- Exchanges, consolidations and every transaction without a choice.
- `review.replayed_lots` (`review.py:651`), which replays an exchange
  account's lots oldest first for `sale_may_differ_from_1099da`: an
  exchange's own outflows never carry a choice, and coins a wallet chose
  to send to an exchange arrive there as lots like any others.
- Form 8949's boxes (`form_8949.py`): a wallet's disposals are I or L
  (C or F before 2025) whatever coins they take; a choice moves a row
  between short- and long-term only through its lot's date.

## 6. Every way in and out

### 6.1 The form's picker (on #706's preview)

#706 (the owner's decision there, 2026-10-25; fix slot B, branch
`2.0-fix-706`) has the form list, before a Transfer between two accounts
saves, the coins that will move, from the server's own save run on a
scratch copy (`services/coins_moved.py`, `POST
/api/transactions/coins-moved` and `/{id}/coins-moved`, as its checklist
names them; C3 uses whatever names it lands with). C3 builds on it:

- **The server's answer grows** (`coins_moved.py`):
  - it covers a bitcoin Withdrawal from a wallet as well as a Transfer:
    the coins that leave (each piece's date acquired, BTC, cost, and
    whether it pays the fee);
  - for a wallet, `held`: the coins the wallet holds when the transaction
    comes, before it takes any, one line per acquisition (its id, how it
    was acquired and where, date acquired or INHERITED, BTC held then,
    their cost, and the term they'd have on this date), and which of them
    oldest first would take;
  - it captures `held` in the replay itself, at the moment the
    transaction posts its lots (a hook like the replay's `DEADLINE` in
    `db.info`), so the picker, the preview and the save can't disagree;
  - its dry run always replays the whole ledger on the scratch copy, so
    every lot knows its acquisition (call (j)).
- **The picker** (a new `transactionForm/ChosenCoins.tsx`, shown by
  `TransferFields` and `WithdrawalFields` when 2.2 says yes):
  - #706's list and line by default: the oldest coins, the IRS's default;
  - a "Choose which coins leave" button opens one line per acquisition
    held, each with a BTC field, filled with oldest first;
  - quick fills (Q3): "Oldest first" and "Highest cost first";
  - a running total: "Chosen 0.01010000 of 0.01010000 BTC (0.01000000
    sent, 0.00010000 network fee)", and Save waits until it adds up;
  - the rule's words (7.1) beside it, and on an edit, "Chosen on
    <date>";
  - an edit that changes the amount, fee, From or To with a choice shows
    the picker again, its total no longer adding up, so the user chooses
    again or goes back to oldest first;
  - laid out by P7's rules (#572): a table at desktop width, one card per
    acquisition at phone width; Safari 15 CSS only (the Mac app's
    WebKit).
- **The Transactions list** marks a row whose coins were chosen ("Coins
  chosen"), with the coins in its detail.

### 6.2 The past-year question

Nothing new is needed for it to ask. A choice is part of the write's data
(`past_years.Write.data`), so the dry run applies it, the fingerprint
covers it (`Write.parts`), and `year_changes` sees what it moves: the
disposals' rows, boxes and column (b), the transaction's basis and gain.
A choice dated in this tax year saves at once, like any write dated
there. One line is added to the 409 (`past_years`) and its dialog
(`PastYearDialog.tsx`) when the write adds or changes a choice on a
transaction dated in an earlier tax year: "Coins chosen after they moved
don't count as choosing them: the IRS then counts the oldest coins as
the ones that moved (Treas. Reg. §1.1012-1(j)(2))."

### 6.3 The CSV export and import

BitcoinTX's own CSV imports into an empty ledger only
(`csv_import.py`'s docstring), in the export's order, so a row can point
at another row of the same file:

- two columns appended to `CSV_COLUMNS` (`csv_import.py:87`, optional, so
  older files import as before): `chosen_coins`, written `row 5:
  0.01000000; row 9: 0.00500000`, each the acquisition's row as the
  import numbers rows (the header is row 1, `csv_import.py:226`); and
  `coins_chosen_at` (ISO UTC);
- the export (`routers/backup.py`, `_export_row`) writes each
  acquisition's row number in its own output;
- the import maps each referenced row to the transaction it creates, and
  refuses, naming the row, a reference to a row that isn't a Buy or
  Deposit into a bitcoin account, or isn't in the file; a blank
  `coins_chosen_at` with chosen coins takes the import's time;
- the template's example rows gain one Spent with chosen coins;
- an older BitcoinTX ignores the two columns and imports the rows oldest
  first, so its figures differ: the CSV section of the user guide says
  so (C6).

### 6.4 River's import and the exchange importer: oldest first

They write an exchange's rows (`river_import.py`; every other exchange's
file is read into River's rows, `exchange_import.py`). One kind leaves a
wallet: bitcoin the exchange received, proposed as a Transfer from the
wallet the user picks (`receive`, `river_import.py:615`). An
exchange's file can't say which coins the wallet sent, so the import
writes no choice: the row takes the oldest first, and the user may choose
coins on it afterwards in the form, like any Transfer. The preview says
so on such a row ("From your wallet, oldest coins first: you can choose
the coins after the import"). C4 adds a test that an imported row never
has `chosen_coins`, and that choosing on one afterwards works.

### 6.5 The AI connector and its guide

Q4 decides whether the AI may record choices. With the recommended
answer:

- **Adding** (`entry_import.py`, the preview and the add): a row leaving a
  wallet takes `chosen_coins`, each acquisition by its transaction id
  in the ledger (a row of the same request can't be named: the AI adds
  the purchase first). The preview's answer says, for each such row, the
  coins it takes and the coins the wallet held then (the same capture as
  6.1), at most 200 acquisitions, oldest first, with a count of any more.
- **Editing** (`update_transaction`): takes `chosen_coins` (a list or
  null); the refusal of call (e) is its answer, like the past-year 409
  (`saved: false` and how to go on). To see an existing transaction's
  coins, the AI calls #706's `POST /api/transactions/{id}/coins-moved`
  through a new tool, added to `AI_KEY_ROUTES` on purpose (a dry run on a
  scratch copy, like the preview; its tool and the list agree, as the
  test checks). Its time limit is the connector's long one
  (`client.LONG_TIMEOUT`, #637).
- **Reading:** `list_transactions` shows each row's `chosen_coins` and
  `coins_chosen_at`, so an AI edit doesn't lose them unknowingly.
- **The guide** (`guide.py`), replacing "it can't record coins the user
  identified" (line 38): see 7.3.
- An older connector (2.0.0a2 and before) sends no `chosen_coins`: its
  edits of a row with a choice are refused by call (e), with the words of
  7.1, until the user updates it.

### 6.6 Ledger Review

## 11. Questions for the owner

Each in plain words, with one recommendation. Q1 and Q2 are needed before
C2, Q3 before C3, Q4 before C5, Q5 before C6.

**Q1. Choosing coins for a transaction that's already in the past.**
People often record a send days later. The IRS only counts a choice you
made by the time the coins left, and BitcoinTX can't tell when you
decided. Options:
- (a) allow it; the picker always shows the rule; BitcoinTX records the
  day the choice was saved, lists choices saved after the coins left in
  Ledger Review, and prints that day in the tax report;
- (b) the same, plus a tick box, "I chose these coins by the time they
  left", that must be ticked when the choice is saved after that day;
- (c) refuse choices saved more than a few days after the coins left.

**Recommendation: (a).** BitcoinTX can't check either way; the saved date
and the listing are the honest record a tax professional reads, and coin
control in a wallet is a real choice made at the time even when it's
entered later. A tick box adds a step to the common case without adding
proof, and (c) would refuse people whose wallet records the choice.

**Q2. A change to an earlier transaction that takes away coins a later
one chose.** Say a later spend chose the coins from your March 2024
purchase, and you then delete that purchase, or lower its amount.
Options:
- (a) refuse the change, and say which later transaction to change first;
- (b) ask, and on yes save it and set that later transaction back to
  oldest first.

**Recommendation: (a).** Nothing you chose changes behind your back, and
the message says exactly what to do. (b) could follow later if (a) proves
tiresome.

**Q3. Quick fills in the picker.** Besides typing amounts per purchase:
- (a) "Oldest first" (the default) and "Highest cost first";
- (b) only "Oldest first";
- (c) more: lowest cost first, newest first.

**Recommendation: (a).** Highest cost first is the usual reason to
choose (the smallest gain), and the IRS names it ((j)(4)). What's saved
is still the coins themselves, so later edits never re-run a rule. More
buttons make the form busier for rare needs.

**Q4. The AI connector.**
- (a) It may record coins you tell it you chose at the time, and can show
  which coins a wallet held; its guide tells it never to suggest choosing
  coins after the fact to lower tax;
- (b) it only reads choices; you choose coins in the form.

**Recommendation: (a).** Much of the entry goes through the AI, and
someone who says "I sent the coins I bought in 2024" should be recorded
as they said. The guide's rule keeps it to what you decided at the time.

**Q5. The words.** Decision 30's full sentence gains its ending:
"Cost basis is per-wallet FIFO: each wallet and exchange uses its own
oldest coins first for a sale, spend or transfer, unless you choose other
coins in one of your own wallets." The tax report's method line, in a
year where you chose coins: "Per-wallet FIFO, with the coins you chose in
your wallets".

**Recommendation: these words.** They keep decision 30's sentence whole,
say where a choice is possible (your own wallets) and where it isn't
(exchanges, by leaving them out).
