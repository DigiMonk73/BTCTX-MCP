**In plain words (P1):** BitcoinTX already keeps each of its two bitcoin
accounts, Wallet and Exchange BTC, as its own pile of coins, oldest first.
So 2.0 isn't a rewrite of the tax engine. What's fixed to "one of each" is
spread wide but shallow: the rules for which account a Buy or Sell may use,
about twenty spots that ask "is this Wallet or Exchange BTC?" by number,
the imports, the AI connector, the screens and the texts. The big risk for
someone who already uses BitcoinTX is history: moving old transactions into
a new account changes the figures of years already filed (section 2, C2).
The IRS rules (section 3) match what 2.0 wants: each wallet and each
exchange account is its own pile, first in, first out, by the date the
coins were first acquired.

**How this was made:** every claim was checked in the code on `develop`
or read from the IRS's own documents (3.1), and four were run on throwaway
databases (2, C2; 1.1; 1.4; 1.12). A fresh reviewer checked about 150
references and the IRS quotes (its review is on #290). P1 wrote it at
`develop` 4a4f587; **P2 refreshed every line number and fact to `develop`
9fb764f** (2026-10-15), after #286, #288, #282 and #293, #291 (#285: the
CSV preview calls the save's own checks, which added two checks by
account number, 1.4), #295 (#100: a lot's cost claimed as a running
total), #300 (#297), #301 and #302 (#296: Ledger Review compares a
transaction's rows) and #303 (#298: a USD fee in cents, through a new

### 1.3 The engine: what is already per account

Checked in `backend/services/transaction.py`; these need no change of idea,
only the new rules above.

- **Lots belong to an account through the transaction that made them.**
  `BitcoinLot` has no account column (`models/transaction.py:294-365`); a
  lot is the account's when its creating transaction's `to_account_id` is
  that account (`_open_lots`, `:1155-1166`; the year-end holdings read it
  the same way, `services/reports/reporting_core.py:319-320`).
- **FIFO is per account, by the date first acquired.** `_open_lots` takes
  the account's lots with BTC left, ordered by `acquired_date`, then id
  (`:1164`). A Sell or Withdrawal takes from its own account's lots only
  (`maybe_dispose_lots_fifo`, `:1074-1119`, `:1106`). Seen on a throwaway
  database: 2, C2.
- **A Transfer moves lots with their date and basis.** `_LotMove`
  (`:1530-1607`) takes the amount from the source's oldest lots and makes
  new lots in the destination with each source lot's `acquired_date`,
  `inherited` flag and its piece of the lot's cost (`take_from`,
  `:1564-1579`: `piece_cost`, `:1193-1196`, the lot's cost claimed as a
  running total, #100, so a lot's pieces add up to its cost;
  `add_destination_lots`, `:1596-1607`, writes it). The fee's part is a
  disposal (`dispose_fee`, `:1581-1594`, its value shared by
  `running_share`, #297).
- **A Transfer to the same account records only its fee** (#159,
  `_fee_leaving_account`, `:1518-1527`; `:1509-1510`), and needs one
  (`_enforce_fee_rules`, `:2130-2131`).
- **Most checks already go by the account's currency**, not its id: the
  ledger lines (`build_ledger_entries_for_transaction`, `:886-912`), lot
  creation (`maybe_create_bitcoin_lot`, `:1030-1032`), disposal
  (`:1084-1086`), transfers (`:1488-1492`), `_into_btc` (`:1440-1442`), the
  type requirements (`_check_type_requirements`, `:1999-2024`), the date
  originally acquired (`_takes_original_date`, `:2027-2029`; the check,
  `:2056-2083`), fee currency (`_check_fee_currency`, `:1948-1962`;
  `_fill_fee_currency`, `:2086-2102`; `_enforce_fee_rules`, `:2117-2162`;
  `_default_btc_transfer_fee`, `:1670-1678`). Since #291 the amount and
  fee checks are shared helpers that take currencies, not ids
  (`amount_problem`, `fee_currency_problem`, `transfer_fee_problem`, and
  since #303 `fee_currency_used` and `fee_cents_problem`, `:1825-1874`),
  and the CSV preview calls them too; the CSV side still
  works out those currencies by id (1.4).
- **The fee accounts are two, shared by every account** (`_fee_account`,
  `:929-931`, by name). Fees are taken from the paying account's own lots
  either way; the fee accounts only hold the fee's ledger line.
- **Balances are per account already** (`services/calculation.py:49-68`,
  every row of the accounts table).
- **The recalculation replays everything in time order**
  (`recalculate_all_transactions`, `:1610-1635`): any change to an old
  transaction can move any later figure, in any year. True in 1.x too; 2.0
  adds more ways to do it (2, C2).
- Messages that name the fixed accounts (shown to users and passed on by
  the AI): `:1484-1485` (docstring), `:2046-2047` ("…into Wallet or
  Exchange BTC."), `:2169-2188` and the docstring at `:2195-2199`.

## 2. Consequences for someone who already uses BitcoinTX

Each with an example. "Filed years" means tax years whose forms the user
may already have sent.

**C1. Upgrading a 1.x database changes nothing by itself.** Ids 1–6 and 99
stay (owner, #251 decision 4); lots are found through the transaction that
made them (1.3), so no lot moves. A migration adds what the accounts table
needs (a kind, the exchange pair) and fills it for ids 1–6. Test: the
reference figures and the independent check agree before and after.
Example: a 1.x ledger with Wallet and Exchange BTC lots gives the same
Form 8949 rows after the upgrade. The exceptions are the old databases of
1.12: accounts other than 1–6 made through the API, and (from before
v0.9.2) transactions naming an account that doesn't exist, which must not
come alive in a new account (Q3).

**C2. Moving an old transaction into another account changes filed years.**
Run on a throwaway database (`develop` 5fb6ac5), with Exchange BTC standing
in for a second wallet, which FIFO treats the same way:

| | Wallet holds | The 2024-05-01 spend of 0.5 BTC for $30,000 |
|---|---|---|
| As entered | 1 BTC from 2020-03-01 ($5,000) and 1 BTC from 2023-06-01 ($25,000) | basis $2,500, gain **$27,500, long-term** (the 2020 coins) |
| The 2020 deposit edited into another account | only the 2023 coins | basis $12,500, gain **$17,500, short-term** |

The 2024 return already filed would be wrong by both amount and term. 1.x
can do this too (edit a deposit from Wallet to Exchange BTC), but 2.0 invites
it: a user who pooled two wallets in 1.x and now adds the second one will
want to "put the old deposits where they really went". Q4, Q5.

**C3. Splitting a pooled wallet or exchange.** A 1.x user with two wallets
recorded both as Wallet (README, "One wallet, one exchange"). In 2.0, if
the new wallet starts empty and coins move into it by a Transfer dated when
they actually moved, the Transfer takes the **oldest** coins of Wallet
(FIFO), not necessarily the coins that were really in the second wallet;
past years stay as filed (pooled). Rewriting the history instead (C2)
changes filed years. Either way, figures for 2025 on (when the IRS rule
applies, 3.2) can differ from a per-wallet calculation from the start. The
app can't know which coins were where; Q5 decides what it offers and says.

**C4. Moves between two wallets or two exchanges.** In 1.x they are a
Transfer to the same account, recording only the network fee (#159: the
coins stay in their lots). In 2.0 a move between two tracked accounts is an
ordinary Transfer: the coins leave the source oldest first and keep their
dates and basis; the fee is a disposal, as today. Old same-account rows
keep their 1.x meaning unless the user edits them (C2). Example: 0.3 BTC
Wallet → Wallet with a 0.0001 fee: today a fee disposal only; in 2.0, Wallet
A → Wallet B, also 0.2999 BTC of lots move to B.

**C5. Buys and Sells on a second exchange.** A Buy takes dollars from a
bank or that exchange's own dollars and puts the coins in that exchange's
BTC account; a Sell is that exchange's BTC to its own dollars. Until
`form_8949._broker_reporting` (1.5) changes, a Sell from a second exchange
would be treated as not broker-reported (box I/L instead of H/K, or G/J
from 2026): the gain is the same, the box and Schedule D line are wrong. So
the forms change before the gate opens (Q17).

**C6. Fees.** Unchanged in idea: a Buy's or Sell's USD fee is the trade's
(basis or proceeds); a BTC network fee is a disposal from the paying
account's oldest coins. The two fee accounts stay shared (1.3).

**C7. Income, rebates, inherited coins and gifts into a new wallet.** The
engine values them by currency (1.3), but the reports, the dashboard,
Ledger Review, the hourly check, the AI import's autofill and the CSV
export ask for ids 2 or 4 (1.4). If the gate opened first, **income into a
new wallet would be missing from the tax report and the dashboard**, and a
gift from it would keep a blank value. So 1.4 is fixed before the gate
opens.

**C8. The 2025+ boxes and covered lots, per exchange.** Today: covered
(G/J) only for a lot bought on the one exchange from 2026-01-01 and sold
there without moving. In 2.0: covered only when bought and sold in the
**same** exchange account; coins moved from exchange A to exchange B are
noncovered at B (3.5), so a Sell of them at B is H/K (proceeds only). A
user who in 1.x set `broker_reporting` to "proceeds" on such a Sell (the
guide's advice, `guide.py:142-144`) keeps that setting: it wins over the
rules (`form_8949.py:216-218`), and gives the same box.

**C9. Year-end holdings.** Already listed per lot with the account's name
(1.5): the new accounts show by name. The start-of-year list has no account
column (add it). The year-end value uses one price for every account
(unchanged).

**C10. Ledger Review.** Its checks miss new accounts until 1.4 is fixed;
its items name no account (1.6).

**C11. Backups, restore, and going back to 1.x.** A 1.x backup restores
into 2.0 (upgraded first). A 2.0 backup can't be restored into 1.x (refused
as newer, 1.7), and 1.x won't even start on a database 2.0 has migrated
(`backend/migrate.py:407-412`). **Going back from 2.0 to 1.x means using a
copy made before the upgrade, losing what was entered since**, and that
copy isn't simple to use:

- The upgrade's own copy (`migrate.backup_sqlite`, `backend/migrate.py:135`:
  `<data dir>/backups/<name>-before-<label>-<time>.db`) is a plain database
  file. The in-app restore takes only BitcoinTX's encrypted backups
  (`backend/services/backup.py:2, 96-139`), so going back means swapping the
  file by hand (`docs/MAINTENANCE.md:312-313`), or on StartOS restoring a
  StartOS backup (`startos/instructions.md`, "Limitations").
- Pre-upgrade and pre-restore copies share one kind, "before", which keeps
  only its newest 5 (`BACKUPS_KEPT`, `backend/migrate.py:47`; the docstring
  at `:136-143`): later upgrades or in-app restores push the pre-2.0 copy
  out.

P2 designs the rollback of each step; Q21 asks how a user goes back.

**C12. CSV export and re-import.** A new account exports a blank name
today (1.7). In 2.0 the export writes each account's name, and the import
reads it back; a CSV exported from 1.x (names Bank, Wallet, Exchange USD,
Exchange BTC, External) must still import (Q9). If names can be changed
(Q2), a CSV exported before a rename names an account that no longer
exists.

**C13. The AI connector.** The connector on PyPI has the five names
compiled into its tool schemas (1.8): **a 1.x connector can't name a new
account**, and its guide tells the AI to pool. A 2.0 connector must read
the accounts from the server; whether it still works with a 1.x server is
Q10. A connector installed from an older release keeps its names and its
guide until it is updated, so the server's own checks must decide, never
the connector's.

**C14. The reference test ledger and its figures.** Adding second-account
rows moves the figures of the years they're in. The goal set in #251 (the
lead's analysis) is every existing row's figures unchanged (AGENTS.md
"Testing" only says to add rows and run `make reference-figures`, which
accepts a meant change): the new rows should use only new accounts, or move
coins out of an existing account only after its last disposal (Q18).

**C15. The StartOS package and Start9.** The store descriptions, the
instructions' Limitations and the release notes all say "one wallet, one
exchange" (1.11). 2.0's release changes them in five languages, and Start9
reviews a large change; nothing changes in the package before then.

**C16. The Mac app** holds the owner's real ledger. It upgrades the same
way (C1); the owner's own test of 2.0 must happen on a copy, never the real
data.

### 3.1 The sources, read on 2026-10-15

- Treas. Reg. §1.1012-1(h) and (j): eCFR, current text.
- Treas. Reg. §1.6045-1(a)(15) (covered security), (a)(25) (wallets) and
  (d)(2)(ii)(B) (the broker's lot order); §1.6045A-1(a)(1)(vi) (transfer
  statements): eCFR.
- Rev. Proc. 2024-28 (`irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-24-28.pdf`).
- Notice 2025-7 and Notice 2026-20 (`irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-07.pdf`,
  `n-26-20.pdf`).
- Instructions for Form 8949, 2025 (`irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8949.pdf`, its
  proof line "20-Jan-2026") and Form 8949, 2025.
- Instructions for Form 1099-DA, 2025 (`irs-prior/i1099da--2025.pdf`) and
  2026 (`irs-pdf/i1099da.pdf`, "18-Feb-2026").
- The IRS digital-asset FAQ, Part II (page reviewed 29-Jun-2026): Q81,
  Q86–Q90, Q95, Q96. The older FAQ's answers 40 and 41 (before 2025) are
  cited as Rev. Proc. 2024-28 §2 quotes them, not from that page.

### 3.2 Treas. Reg. §1.1012-1(j): wallet by wallet, from 2025

- **Applies** "to all acquisitions and dispositions of digital assets on or
  after January 1, 2025" ((j)(6)). It comes from §1012(c)(1): the basis
  conventions "must be applied on an account-by-account basis" (as Rev.
  Proc. 2024-28 §2 puts it).
- **Not at a broker** ((j)(1), (j)(2): "such as in a single unhosted wallet
  or in a hosted wallet provided by a person other than a broker"): the
  taxpayer may identify the units sold on their own books "no later than
  the date and time" of the sale. Without that, units go "in order of time
  from the earliest date on which units … were acquired by the taxpayer",
  and "the date any units were transferred into the taxpayer's wallet is
  disregarded". The regulation's Example 2 applies this to the units "held
  in the unhosted wallet" only: FIFO wallet by wallet, by the date first
  acquired.
- **At a broker** ((j)(3)(i), (ii)): the taxpayer identifies units **to
  the broker** (a standing order counts; a broker with only one method
  makes it a standing order), else FIFO by the date first acquired, the
  transfer-in date disregarded. Example 4: the earliest units "held in
  TP's account with BEX", one broker account at a time. It also shows
  that the broker may report different units than the taxpayer must use
  (when the broker had no purchase dates for coins transferred in).
- **A wallet**, per §1.6045-1(a)(25): "a means of storing … a user's
  private keys"; addresses a wallet generates and "a sub-ledger account of
  a wallet" are held in that wallet ((a)(25)(iv)). FAQ Q96: a duplicate
  record of the keys "does not result in a single wallet that holds all of
  the digital assets".
- **Changing the identification method is not a change of accounting
  method** ((j)(4)).

### 3.3 Rev. Proc. 2024-28: the one-time move to wallet-by-wallet

- A **safe harbor** to allocate "unused basis" (the basis of coins still
  held) to the coins in each wallet or account **as of January 1, 2025**
  ("immediately after the close of the taxpayer's day on December 31,
  2024", §3.10). Only for coins held then (§4.01(1)–(2)).
- Two ways: a **specific unit allocation**, completed before the earlier
  of the first sale, disposition or transfer of that asset on or after
  2025-01-01 and the due date (with extensions) of the 2025 return
  (§5.02(4)); or a **global allocation**, a rule written in the taxpayer's
  books **before January 1, 2025** and completed by the later of those two
  dates (§5.02(5)). "Transfer" here means to **another taxpayer**, a gift
  included (§3.11): moving coins between one's own wallets isn't one. Allocations are "irrevocable" (§4.02(6)); basis
  already used can't be allocated again (§5.02(7)).
- The background (§2): many taxpayers had used a "universal or
  multi-wallet approach" before 2025, which the old FAQ didn't forbid.

### 3.4 Notices 2025-7 and 2026-20: identification for coins at a broker

Through **December 31, 2026** (Notice 2026-20, §3.03), units held at a broker
may be identified on the taxpayer's **own books** (a choice before the sale,
or a standing order written in the books), and then those are the units sold
"regardless of whether the information reported by the broker to the
taxpayer matches the taxpayer's books and records" (§4.05). Taxpayers using
Rev. Proc. 2024-28 may rely on it only after meeting its requirements
(§4.04). From 2027, identification of exchange-held coins goes to the
broker again ((j)(3)(ii)): a standing order counts, and a broker that
offers only one method makes that method the standing order (3.2);
without it, FIFO per broker account.

### 3.5 Covered lots and the broker's form (Form 1099-DA)

- **Covered** (§1.6045-1(a)(15)(i)(J)): "acquired in a customer's account
  by a broker providing custodial services … on or after January 1, 2026,
  in exchange for cash, stored-value cards, different digital assets, or
  any other property or services described in paragraph (a)(9)(ii)(B) or
  (C)" (a purchase, in short: not, for example, interest or rewards the
  exchange pays). The 1099-DA instructions (2025, p. 2; 2026, p. 1) add
  that it is covered "only if it was acquired in an account for which the
  broker provided custodial services and was held in that account until
  the broker effects the disposition". **Noncovered** includes "A digital
  asset that was transferred in to the broker providing custodial
  services" and anything acquired before 2026.
- **Who files it:** "Generally, only a U.S. digital asset broker is
  required to report on Form 1099-DA" (the 1099-DA instructions on
  irs.gov, "Brokers", read 2026-10-15). An exchange outside the US
  usually sends none.
- **No transfer statement is required** between brokers for digital assets
  (§1.6045A-1(a)(1)(vi)), so coins moved from one exchange to another
  arrive noncovered (the covered-by-transfer-statement route,
  §1.6045-1(a)(15)(i)(G), doesn't arise unless a broker sends one).
- **2025:** brokers report gross proceeds; "Brokers are not required to
  report basis information with respect to sales effected in 2025" (2025
  instructions, p. 1).
- **The broker's own lot order** (2026 instructions, p. 8): the customer's
  identification, else "the earliest units of the digital asset purchased
  by the customer"; without customer-provided acquisition information,
  "treat digital assets transferred into the customer's account as
  acquired as of the date and time of the transfer", and such information
  is used "solely for lot-selection purposes and not for reporting basis
  or acquisition dates". So a broker's 1099-DA can differ from the
  taxpayer's own FIFO by original dates (#158).

### 3.6 Form 8949 (2025)

- Digital assets use boxes G, H, I (short) and J, K, L (long), never C or
  F (instructions, p. 1 and p. 4); "Use a separate Part I for each type",
  "Check only one box on each Part I" (p. 3–4).
- Multiple brokers: the instructions ask for nothing per broker when each
  transaction is on its own row. Only Exception 2 (summary totals instead
  of rows) says "If you have statements from more than one broker, report
  the totals from each broker on a separate row" (p. 4). BitcoinTX lists
  every disposal, so it doesn't use Exception 2.
- Column (b) may say "VARIOUS" for units bought on several dates, but
  short- and long-term still go on separate parts (p. 6); "INHERITED" for
  inherited property (p. 6).

### 3.7 What this means for BitcoinTX

**Already followed by 1.x (for its two bitcoin accounts):** FIFO within
each account by the date first acquired, the transfer date disregarded
(1.3); a transferred lot keeps its date and basis; coins moved between
one's own accounts aren't sold, but the fee paid in coins is (FAQ Q81); the
2025+ boxes; covered only for coins bought on the exchange from 2026 and
never moved (1.5).

**2.0 must follow:** the same, for every wallet and every exchange account
separately; covered per exchange account (bought and sold in the same one;
moved between exchanges = noncovered); each exchange's Sells
broker-reported by that exchange; income and gifts recognized whatever the
account (1.4).

**2.0 must not promise:**

- **Choosing which coins to sell** (specific identification), in a wallet
  or at an exchange: FIFO only, as in 1.x (#251). Through 2026 a user may
  identify units on their own books (3.4), and a broker may apply a
  standing order other than FIFO; BitcoinTX's figures then differ, and
  the report should say so. **For wallets, the owner's decision 29
  (2026-10-25, #711) builds it into 2.0** (`docs/temp/2.0-coin-choice.md`);
  exchanges stay FIFO.
- **Tracking coins by address or UTXO.**
- **The Rev. Proc. 2024-28 allocation.** Its deadlines (3.3): the
  specific-unit way closed at the first 2025 sale, disposition, or gift to
  someone else (moves between one's own wallets don't count), if that came
  before the 2025 return's due date; the global way needed its rule written
  before 2025, and must be completed by the later of the first such sale,
  disposition or gift and the 2025 return's due date (with extensions). The
  specific-unit way is closed for everyone once that due date has passed.
  It is the taxpayer's own, irrevocable
  choice, made with a tax professional, not in the app.
  BitcoinTX's per-account lots as of 2025-01-01 are whatever its history
  gives.
- **That past years were right** for a user who pooled multiple wallets in
  1.x: those years used one pile for what were multiple wallets (README,
  "One wallet, one exchange"). Before 2025 the IRS's FIFO default (FAQ 41)
  didn't say "per wallet", and many read it as across all holdings (the
  "universal" approach, Rev. Proc. 2024-28 §2), while records per wallet
  could support identification (FAQ 40); which one a user's returns relied
  on is for their tax professional.
- **That BitcoinTX matches each 1099-DA.** Brokers may use other lot dates
  (3.5); #158 is where a broker's figures are entered.

## 4. Open questions for P2

Each has one recommendation. P2 puts them to the owner in one round.

**Q1. What is an account?** Recommendation: three kinds the user adds:
**bank** (USD), **wallet** (BTC), and **exchange**, which is always a pair,
its dollars and its bitcoin, as two rows in the accounts table tied
together (so a Buy at one exchange can't spend another's dollars). External
(99) and the two fee accounts stay as they are, internal.

**Q2. Can accounts be renamed?** Recommendation: yes, every account the
user has (Bank, Wallet and Exchange included), by a name kept unique
ignoring case. The seven 1.x names (Bank, Wallet, Exchange USD, Exchange
BTC, BTC Fees, USD Fees, External) stay reserved for their own ids, so no
other account can take one, and a file that says "Wallet" always means id
2, renamed or not (Q9). Both resets of ids 1–4's names stop
(`seed_defaults` and `ensure_special_accounts_exist`, 1.1); the fee
accounts keep theirs.

**Q3. Ids for new accounts, and old databases.** Recommendation: new
accounts get ids above 99 and above every id any account or transaction
already uses, so neither External nor an old unknown id (1.12) is ever
taken. Accounts other than 1–6 already in a 1.x database are kept, with
the kind their currency gives them (USD a bank, BTC a wallet: never an
exchange, which is a pair), so nothing moves; the owner can rename or hide
them. Transactions naming an id with no account (from before v0.9.2) stay
as they are, doing nothing, and Ledger Review lists them for the owner to
move to a real account. The upgrade never stops for either.

**Q4. Editing an old transaction into another account.** Recommendation:
allowed, but before it saves, the app shows what it would change in each
year (Ledger Review's `recalc_changes` already computes this) and makes a
backup copy; years that change are named in plain words.

**Q5. A user who pooled multiple wallets or exchanges in 1.x.**
Recommendation: a new account starts empty and gets coins by a Transfer
dated when they moved (FIFO from the source; dates and basis kept); the
app never re-assigns history by itself, and says once, when the second
wallet or exchange is added, that years before it were figured pooled and
that a tax professional can say whether anything needs amending. No
Rev. Proc. 2024-28 tool (3.7).

**Q6. Covered lots and broker reporting per exchange.** Recommendation: a
Sell from any exchange's BTC account is broker-reported by that exchange;
covered when its lot was made by a Buy into that same exchange's BTC
account on or after 2026-01-01 (moved coins are never covered); the
per-transaction `broker_reporting` override stays and wins.

**Q7. Forms and the report per account.** Recommendation: Form 8949 stays
one set of sheets per box, a row per disposal, column (a) unchanged (3.6);
the Complete Tax Report adds the account (and exchange) to each disposal
and to the start-of-year holdings, so the user can match each exchange's
1099-DA.

**Q8. The River import.** Recommendation: the import asks once per file
which exchange it is (default: the first exchange, today's Exchange) and
which wallet untagged sends and receives go to (default: the first
wallet), each row still editable in the preview.

**Q9. Accounts in CSV files.** Recommendation: keep names (the format is
for people), each account's current name on export; the import reads the
user's names ignoring case, the reserved 1.x names always as their own
ids (Q2), and refuses others with the list of valid names. A 1.x CSV
imports into 2.0 unchanged.

**Q10. The AI connector.** Recommendation: the server lists the accounts
to the AI key (a read-only route added to `AI_KEY_ROUTES` on purpose, with
id, name, kind and currency); the connector checks names against it at
run time instead of a fixed list, and `get_portfolio` includes it (no new
tool, so the tool count stays 11). Against a 1.x server (no list) the
connector falls back to today's five names.

**Q11. Who adds accounts.** Recommendation: only the owner, logged in
(Settings), like the other settings; the AI key can't create, rename or
hide one. The guide tells the AI to ask the user to add a missing wallet or
exchange in BitcoinTX, never to pool it.

**Q12. Deleting and changing accounts.** Recommendation: an account no
transaction names can be deleted; one in use can only be hidden from the
pickers (its history stays); its currency and kind never change once used;
ids 1–4 can be renamed (Q2), the fee accounts can't, and none of 1–6 can
be deleted. The fee accounts' gap in 1.x (#289) is worth its small fix
now, in 1.x (done: #330, on `develop` for 1.2.9).

**Q13. One answer to "is it a bitcoin account?".** Recommendation: one
helper that reads the account's currency, replacing `BTC_ACCOUNTS` in all
of 1.4 (and the frontend's `id === 1 || id === 3`), done before the gate
opens.

**Q14. Transfers between any two accounts.** Recommendation: any two of
the user's accounts in the same currency (bank to bank, one exchange's
dollars to another's, wallet to wallet, exchange to wallet and back); a
Transfer to the same account stays only for a BTC account's consolidation.

**Q15. Buys.** Recommendation: a Buy is paid from a bank or from that same
exchange's dollars, and lands in that exchange's BTC account; a Buy paying
with one exchange's dollars into another's bitcoin is refused.

**Q16. The dashboard.** Recommendation: one row per account, an exchange's
dollars and bitcoin together, and today's totals (total BTC, average cost,
gains) unchanged.

**Q17. Order of the build.** Recommendation: data model and migration;
then 1.4 and the forms (1.5) made general and tested with the gate still
closed; then the imports, export and connector; then the transaction
form's edit keeping each row's accounts (1.9: today it rewrites a Buy's
and a Sell's); then the gate; then the rest of the screens; then the
texts (1.11). Each step leaves 1.x users' figures
unchanged (the reference figures and the independent check).

**Q18. Test data.** Recommendation: make `scripts/independent_figures.py`
general (a pile per account, covered per exchange) before the engine
changes; then add rows to `testdata/reference_ledger.json` that use new
accounts only, or that move coins out of an existing account only after
its last disposal there, so every existing row's figures stay the same
(the goal set in #251).

**Q19. What a wallet is.** Recommendation: the user decides; the help text
gives the IRS's meaning (3.2: one set of keys, its addresses included) and
says that a backup of the same keys isn't a second wallet.

**Q20. What 1.x users see after upgrading.** Recommendation: the same
three accounts and the same figures; the About notice changes from "one
wallet, one exchange" to how to add one, with Q5's note about years
figured pooled.

**Q21. Going back to 1.x.** Recommendation: the upgrade to 2.0 keeps its
copy of the 1.x database apart from the five rotating "before" copies, and
never removes it by itself (C11); before 2.0 ships, the docs say how to go
back on each platform: on StartOS, restore the StartOS backup made before
the update; on Docker and the Mac app, stop BitcoinTX and put that copy in
place of the database. Either way, what was entered in 2.0 is lost.

## 5. Design (P2)

The owner accepted Q1–Q21 as recommended (2026-10-15, on #292). This
section turns them into the design: what is stored, the rules, the forms,
the gate, the imports, the screens, the tests, the rollback and a rough
order of build steps. P3 tries to break it; P4 cuts it into build steps.
New names (tables, columns, functions, messages) are this design's
proposals: a build step may rename one, but not change its rule without
going back through the lead. Line numbers are `develop` 94cfe52 (P3's
refresh; P2 wrote it at 9fb764f).

**In plain words:** today's three accounts stay exactly as they are, with
the same numbers and the same figures. You can add a bank, a wallet or an
exchange in Settings; an exchange always comes with its dollars and its
bitcoin. Every check that today asks "is this the Wallet or the Exchange?"
asks instead "does this account hold bitcoin?". A sale on an exchange is
reported as that exchange's sale. Nothing rewrites your history by
itself: a new wallet starts empty, and coins move into it by a Transfer.
If a change would alter the figures of a tax year you may already have
filed (adding a backdated move, editing or deleting a transaction,
changing the tax timezone), BitcoinTX first shows which years and makes
a copy of your ledger. Each
exchange has a switch for whether it sends you a Form 1099-DA, which
decides the box on the tax form. The upgrade keeps a copy of your 1.x ledger that BitcoinTX
never deletes, so you can go back (on StartOS, by its own backup).
It's built in ten steps, and after each one a 1.x ledger's figures are
checked to the cent.

### 5.1 Definitions

**Your accounts** are the rows of the accounts table of kind bank, wallet
or exchange (Q1):

| Kind | Currency | What it is | Today's |
|---|---|---|---|
| bank | USD | a bank account, or any dollar account outside an exchange | Bank (1) |
| wallet | BTC | a self-custody wallet: one set of keys and every address it makes (Q19) | Wallet (2) |
| exchange | USD and BTC | an exchange account: always two rows, its dollars and its bitcoin, tied to one exchange | Exchange USD (3) and Exchange BTC (4), of exchange 1, "Exchange" |
| fee | BTC, USD | internal: holds the fee lines; no transaction names it | BTC Fees (5), USD Fees (6) |
| — | — | External: everything outside BitcoinTX; not a row, as today | External (99) |

- **A bitcoin account** is one of your accounts whose currency is BTC: a
  wallet, or an exchange's BTC account. A fee account isn't one, nor is
  External. **A dollar account** is a bank or an exchange's USD account.
- **An exchange** is a row of a new `exchanges` table (5.2). Its two
  accounts are always named "*exchange name* USD" and "*exchange name*
  BTC": renaming the exchange renames both, and hiding or deleting it does
  both. Exchange 1 is "Exchange", so its accounts keep their 1.x names.
- **A wallet** is what the user says it is (Q19). The help text, exactly:
  "A wallet is one set of keys: every address it makes is part of it. A
  backup of the same keys (a seed phrase, or another device with the same
  keys) isn't a second wallet. If you use multiple wallets, add each one:
  since 2025 the IRS figures cost basis wallet by wallet."
- **Ids** (Q3): 1–6 and 99 never change (#251, decision 4). A new account
  gets 1 + the highest of: 99; every id in the accounts table; every
  account id a transaction (`from_account_id`, `to_account_id`) or a
  ledger line names; and the last id ever given, which the account
  service writes to `app_settings` (`accounts_last_id`) each time it
  makes an account (never the migration: 5.3; a restore keeps the higher
  of the backup's and the live database's, so a restored older backup
  can't hand out an id again, 5.11). So the first new account is 100, no new account
  can take External's 99 or an id an old row names (1.12), and a deleted
  account's id is never given again: a page left open on an old list
  can't save into a different account. The id is worked out and the
  account (or an exchange's two) inserted under the database's write
  lock, taken through the one helper every such write uses since #438,
  `database.take_write_lock` (as Delete All takes it, `services/wipe.py:88`
  at `develop` 1eb4cff): two tabs adding
  at once would otherwise both read the same highest id, and the second
  insert would fail as a server error (P3, 6). Exchanges count their own ids,
  which no client sends: the API and every screen, file and tool name
  accounts by account id or name only.
- **Names** (Q2): an account's or an exchange's name is one line with no
  control character (the engine's rule, `TEXT_ON_ONE_LINE`,
  `transaction.py:1735`), and, stricter than the engine's
  `has_control_character` (`:1993-1996`, which refuses Unicode
  categories Cc, Zl and Zp), no format character either (category Cf:
  the zero-width space U+200B, the bidirectional controls U+202A–U+202E
  and U+2066–U+2069, and the rest), except the zero-width non-joiner
  and joiner (U+200C, U+200D), which some scripts and emoji need.
  Otherwise "Wallet" with a zero-width space after it would pass as a
  name of its own that looks exactly like the reserved "Wallet", and a
  right-to-left override would show a name backwards in the report (P3,
  6). Letters that only look alike (a Cyrillic "а" for a Latin "a") still
  pass: only the owner names accounts, and the comparison is for files
  and the AI. A name may start with an apostrophe (the owner's decision
  23, 2026-10-23, #251: since #380 the CSV import keeps it, `'=Cold`
  coming back as `'=Cold`, so #333's refusal lost its reason). Trimmed, every run
  of spaces (category Zs) made one ASCII space, and
  Unicode-normalised (NFC), 1 to 40
  characters (an exchange's 1 to 36, so "*name* USD" fits). Names are
  compared one way everywhere (the service, the CSV import, River, the
  connector): normalised and `casefold()`ed, what nothing shows left out:
  Unicode's Default_Ignorable_Code_Point characters (the joiners, the
  variation selectors, the combining grapheme joiner, the Hangul fillers)
  and the blank braille pattern (B2a's and B2b's reviews: "Wallet" with
  one after it looks like "Wallet"). A name also loses any at its ends;
  those that are format characters, but the joiners, are refused anyway.
  No two accounts' names are
  alike that way, an exchange's two included, nor two exchanges' names; none is
  "External". **Reserved names:** Bank, Wallet, Exchange USD, Exchange
  BTC, BTC Fees, USD Fees and External always mean ids 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
  and 99 in a file (CSV, River, a 1.x connector's rows; the 2.0 connector
  names a renamed account by its new name, 5.12), whatever those accounts
  are called now, and no other account can take one ("Exchange" is
  exchange 1's). Ids 1–4 can be renamed, and renamed back; 5, 6 never.

### 5.2 Data model

A new table, `exchanges`:

| Column | Type | |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | INTEGER, primary key | 1 is today's exchange |
| `name` | TEXT, NOT NULL, UNIQUE, `COLLATE NOCASE` | "Exchange", "Kraken" |
| `sends_1099da` | BOOLEAN, NOT NULL, default true | "This exchange sends me a Form 1099-DA" (Q6 as the owner refined it, 2026-10-15; 5.7, 5.9) |

Three new columns in `accounts` (`backend/models/account.py`):

| Column | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `kind` | TEXT, NOT NULL once filled (5.3): "bank", "wallet", "exchange" or "fee" | 5.1 |
| `exchange_id` | INTEGER, NULL, refers to `exchanges.id` | set on an exchange's two accounts only |
| `hidden` | BOOLEAN, NOT NULL, default false | left out of the pickers (Q12, 5.9) |

- `name` stays unique and becomes `COLLATE NOCASE`, so the database itself
  refuses two names alike in ASCII case (a restore included, 5.11): a
  backstop, since NOCASE folds only ASCII letters; the rule is 5.1's. `currency`,
  `id` and `user_id` stay as they are. Unique across the install, not per
  user: BitcoinTX has one login (a restore refuses a backup with more,
  `services/backup.py:457-462`), and files and the AI name accounts by
  name, so two accounts can't share one (Q2).
- **No account column on lots.** A lot stays the account's that its
  creating transaction landed in (1.3), and that already scopes every lot
  to one account: a Buy's or a Deposit's lot is in its `to_account_id`; a
  Transfer makes the destination's lots itself (`_LotMove`), so their
  creating transaction's `to_account_id` is the destination; an edit
  rebuilds every lot (the recalculation). FIFO takes only the account's
  own lots (`_open_lots`, `transaction.py:1155-1166`). Adding a column
  would mean migrating every lot, which could move figures, and nothing
  needs it. The balance test made general in 5.15
  (`test_everything.py:483-500`) checks that each bitcoin account's
  balance equals what its own open lots hold.
- `backend/database.py`'s `FIXED_ACCOUNTS` (`:119-126`) gains each row's
  kind and exchange. `seed_defaults` (`:129-166`), at every start and
  after a restore, puts back ids 1–6's currency, kind and exchange,
  exchange 1's row (it makes it on a fresh install, 5.3), and the names of
  5 and 6 only; ids 1–4 keep the names
  the user gave them (Q2). A missing row is made again with its 1.x name
  (for 3 and 4, from exchange 1's current name).
- The second reset, `ensure_special_accounts_exist`
  (`services/account.py:108-135`), and its calls go, so
  `GET /api/accounts` writes nothing (5.12 relies on that).
  `ensure_fee_account_exists` (`transaction.py:863-874`) goes too, and
  `_fee_account` (`:929-931`) finds the fee accounts by id, 5 and 6, not
  by name (#289).

### 5.3 The migration, and old databases

2.0's first migration. **2.0's migrations take ids from 0100** (0100,
0101, …; the planning lead's technical choice within #251's decision 3,
2026-10-18, from P5): 1.x numbers its own on from 0013 and won't reach
them, so 2.0 and 1.x never share an id, which Alembic refuses and a merge
revision can't mend. 2.0's migrations always come after 1.x's in the
chain (#251, decision 3): until alpha.1's publication (the first
pre-release: the owner's decision 28, 2026-10-24), each `develop`
merge that brings a new 1.x migration re-points 2.0's first migration's
`down_revision` to 1.x's newest head, and no file is renamed; after it,
merge revisions (below). At `develop` 94cfe52, and still at 42aa101,
1.x's head is 0012 (`0012_acquired_at_dates.py`, #306), so 2.0's first,
0100, starts on 0012.

1. Create `exchanges`, and insert (1, "Exchange") **only when accounts 3
   or 4 exist** (`INSERT … SELECT 1, 'Exchange' WHERE EXISTS (SELECT 1
   FROM accounts WHERE id IN (3, 4))`). The migration writes no row into
   a fresh database, as no 1.x migration does: a restore builds a fresh
   database by the migrations and then copies the backup's rows in with a
   plain `INSERT … SELECT` (`services/backup.py:303-306`, `_copy_table`
   `:402-403`), so a row the migration had made would clash with the
   backup's and every restore would fail. On a fresh install
   `seed_defaults` makes exchange 1 (5.2).
2. Add `kind` (nullable at first: SQLite adds no NOT NULL column without a
   default, `docs/MAINTENANCE.md`), `exchange_id` and `hidden` (default
   false), and fill them: 1 bank; 2 wallet; 3 and 4 exchange, of exchange
   1; 5 and 6 fee. Any other row (made through the 1.x account API, 1.12)
   is a wallet if its currency is BTC, else a bank: never an exchange,
   which is a pair (Q3). Two kinds of other rows are internal, kind fee,
   never yours, never listed by `GET /api/accounts`: a second "BTC Fees"
   or "USD Fees" (any case) left by #289 in a database from before its
   1.x change in #330, which `ensure_fee_account_exists`
   made after id 5 was renamed and which holds fee lines until the next
   recalculation moves them to 5 (`_fee_account` by id, 5.2); and a row
   with id 99 (possible only after 93 accounts were made through the 1.x
   API), since External is 99 everywhere (5.4).
3. Names: an extra row's name is trimmed, NFC-normalised, has any
   control or format character (5.1) replaced by a space and every run of
   spaces made one (1.x took any text, of any
   length: `schemas/account.py:16`); one left empty becomes "account N",
   and one over 40 characters is cut to fit its suffix; then one whose name is a
   reserved name, or equals another row's name by 5.1's comparison, gets
   " (account N)" added to it, or " (account N, 2)" and so on until the
   name is free, reserved names included, so the rebuild below can't
   fail on a name a ledger already has (between two extra rows, the lower
   id keeps it). The cleaning also drops what nothing shows (the joiners
   and the rest of 5.1's invisible characters) at a name's ends and at a
   cut; it keeps a leading apostrophe (decision 23). Ids 1–6 take their 1.x
   names, which every 1.x start gave them (B2a's review: only a ledger
   #289 stopped starting holds another, and two of them alike would stop
   the rebuild).
   Then, in one batch table copy, `name` becomes `COLLATE NOCASE` and
   `kind` NOT NULL. This also lets a database left by #289 start again:
   its second "BTC Fees" is renamed and 5 gets its name back. A failed
   upgrade, which leaves the 1.x database as it was, removes its pre-2.0
   copy again (nothing else prunes them, and a restart loop would add one
   at each start).

`accounts_last_id` isn't written here (5.1): the next id is computed from
the tables until the first account is made. Nothing else: no transaction, ledger line, lot or disposal is touched,
and nothing is recalculated, so no figure can move (C1). The test: every
stored figure (transactions, ledger lines, lots, disposals) is equal row
by row before and after, on the v0.7.0 fixture, a 1.x database at 1.x's

**The pre-2.0 copy (Q21).** `migrate.upgrade_database`
(`backend/migrate.py:394-446`) makes a copy of the live database before

| Type | From | To | 1.x |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deposit | External | any of your accounts | 99 → 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| Withdrawal | any of your accounts | External | 1, 2, 3, 4 → 99 |
| Transfer | one of your accounts | another of your accounts in the same currency; or the same account, only a bitcoin account (a consolidation: only its network fee is recorded, #159) | 1 ↔ 3, 2 ↔ 4, 2 → 2, 4 → 4 |
| Buy | a bank, or an exchange's USD account | an exchange's BTC account: the same exchange's when paid from an exchange's USD | 1 → 4, 3 → 4 |
| Sell | an exchange's BTC account | the same exchange's USD account | 4 → 3 |

So 2.0 adds: bank to bank, a bank to or from any exchange's USD, one
exchange's USD to another's, wallet to wallet, a wallet to or from any
exchange's BTC, one exchange's BTC to another's, and Buys and Sells on
any exchange. Refused: a Buy paying with one exchange's dollars for
another's bitcoin (Q15), a Sell into another exchange's dollars or a
bank, and any transaction naming a fee account ("Unknown account id 5.",
as today).

**Messages**, exactly, one wording for the API, the form, the CSV, River
and the AI ({…} is an account's current name). They are plain sentences,

### 5.7 Tax forms and the report

The CSV import (it needs an empty ledger), Delete All (its typed
`DELETE` and its copy, #256), a restore (its copy of the live database),
and, with a login, Recalculate Ledger and Ledger Review's
hourly apply (each the owner's own fix, listed first by Ledger Review)
keep their own safeguards. For each covered write:

**When no dry run is needed (P3, 6).** The replay runs in time order
(`recalculate_all_transactions`, `transaction.py:1627`: by timestamp,
then id), so a write can't change anything dated before its earliest
moment: an add's date, and for an edit or a delete the row's date before
and after it. But a save that recalculates also applies every pending
`recalc_changes`, in any year (step 2), so the two shortcuts below hold
only while nothing can be pending: while `recalculated_by` (step 1)
matches. They are most writes:

- **The earliest moment is in the current tax year or later:** no
  earlier year can move, so the write saves as today, with no dry run.
- **An add with nothing dated after it, in an earlier year** (an
  append: today it takes no replay, `_is_backdated`,
  `transaction.py:79`, `:260-277`): no existing figure changes; the row
  only adds its own. It is still a past-year save (the owner's rule), so
  it gets the 409, but its dry run is the save's own path on the scratch
  copy (below), with no replay, and its text says "This adds to tax year
  2025, which you may already have filed" instead of "changes". Entering
  last year's transactions in order is this case only while nothing of
  this year is in the ledger yet: one row dated this year (a January
  recurring buy) makes each such add a backdated one, which takes the
  steps below (#333's review).

When `recalculated_by` doesn't match (the first time after an upgrade,
or after `BTCTX_TIMEZONE` changed), every covered write takes the steps
below, which list the pending changes apart. The 1099-DA switch needs
no replay at all: a box is worked out when a report is built
(`form_8949.py:189-192`) and never stored, so its dry run compares the
boxes of that exchange's Sells under the old and the new setting. Every
other covered write (a backdated add, an edit or a delete reaching back
before this tax year, the tax timezone, the AI's Recalculate Ledger)
goes through these steps:

1. The server works out what the write changes on a scratch copy of
   the database (`database.scratch_copy`, an in-memory copy, moved there
   from `reporting_core` by #361 for #335: the report's year-end replays
   and, since then, Ledger Review's dry run use it), never in the
   live one: a 409 that saves nothing then neither rewrites nor locks the
   ledger. (A session rolled back, as Ledger Review's
   `simulate_recalculation` did before #361,
   held SQLite's write lock for its whole replay, 14 s at 6,000 rows, while every other
   save waits only 5 s: P3, 6.) First, with the lock free, the prices
   the write's own values are filled from are found and stored in the
   live database, as an import does (`transaction.find_prices_first`,
   from #423 for #421): a price the dry run looked up only on the scratch copy would be
   lost with it, and the confirmed save would then ask for it again
   (step 4). It compares a recalculation **without** the edit
   with one **with** it, so changes a recalculation alone would make
   (`recalc_changes`, pending after an upgrade) aren't counted as the
   edit's. The first pass is skipped when the ledger was last
   recalculated by this version of BitcoinTX at this migration head and
   in this tax timezone (a new `recalculated_by` in `app_settings`,
   written only by a full recalculation of the live ledger: not the
   report's partial replays on scratch copies, `reporting_core.py:112,
   173`, nor a dry run's). The version and head keep two builds that share
   a version string apart; the timezone, because the `BTCTX_TIMEZONE`
   fallback (`tax_time.py:35-45`) can change between starts with no
   recalculation. The stored figures are then that pass already, so the
   usual case costs one replay, not two.
   - The edit is `update_transaction_record`'s own path
     (`transaction.py:297-332`: `_check_edit`, the re-pricing of an income
     value whose account changes, the fee, spend and gift re-pricing,
     `_apply_edit`), split from its `db.commit()` (`:330`), so the dry run
     and the save share it. Each pass works on its own copy of the edit's
     data, since the path marks values (a price-filled one would
     otherwise be saved as typed).
   - A **with** pass that fails (a price it can't find, a rule the edit
     breaks) answers as the save would, with the same 400 and message.
   - A **without** pass that fails, on a ledger that can't be replayed as
     it is, is compared no further: the with pass is compared with the
     stored figures instead, and `pending` says that Recalculate Ledger
     can't run now, with its message. Otherwise the edit that repairs such
     a ledger could never be saved. Run by P3 (6): a Transfer naming a
     missing account, from before v0.9.2, stops every replay ("Ledger not
     balanced for BTC"; for 1.x, #337), so editing it into a real account, or deleting
     it, would fail its own without pass for good. In 1.x that edit saves,
     since its replay runs with the row repaired.
   - The comparison is Ledger Review's snapshot (`_snapshot`, `:290-300`)
     plus each disposal's Form 8949 box and its column (b), the date
     acquired or INHERITED (`form_8949.py:181-186`), compared even when
     every dollar matches: two lots of the same cost and term can swap,
     and a covered sale can become noncovered (G/J to H/K) with the same
     gain (C5, C8). The box gets a sentence of its own in the changes'
     text ("Form 8949 box G → H"), since Ledger Review's wording
     (`review.py:468-484`) names money fields only. Beside them (B6b's
     widening above): each row's (source, value) the report
     counts as income or a rebate, (purpose, value) as a gift,
     donation or lost coins, and the date a return prints, in the tax
     timezone, not only its year (`year_changes.figures`).
   - Every transaction whose figures, boxes or dates differ is grouped by
     its tax year (tax timezone).
2. If nothing moves (a dollar-only move, or one with no effect), it saves
   as today. As in 1.x, a save that recalculates (an edit, a delete, an
   add dated before the latest) also applies any pending
   `recalc_changes`, which Ledger Review lists; an add dated after the
   latest applies none (`transaction.py:79-84`, #172). **The owner's
   decision (2026-10-16, in chat with the lead): "Don't warn for
   fixes".** With a login, those pending changes alone don't make a 409:
   they are rule fixes, which Ledger Review lists first, and Recalculate
   Ledger is the owner's own way to apply them (P2's choice, now the
   owner's). **With the AI key they do**, when one is
   in an earlier year: the write answers 409 with them as `pending`, as
   the AI's Recalculate Ledger does. Otherwise any AI edit of a row of
   this year, even unchanged, would apply them unasked (#333's review).
3. If something moves and the request doesn't confirm it, the answer is
   409 and nothing is saved. The body keeps `detail` a plain string, as
   every error's is, and adds the rest:
   `{"detail": "This changes the figures of tax years 2024 and 2025, which you may already have filed. Confirm to save it: a copy of your ledger is made first.", "years": [2024, 2025], "changes": [...], "pending": [...], "confirm": "<fingerprint>"}`.
   - "This changes", not "moving this transaction": the write can be an
     add, an edit of any field, a delete or an import.
   - `changes` are Ledger Review's items (date, type, what changes, as
     `_recalc_items`, `review.py:482`, gives them).
   - `pending` is any `recalc_changes` the save would apply too, shown
     apart ("These also change, from rules fixed since they were saved").
   - `confirm` is a fingerprint (SHA-256) of the write, of what it
     changes and of `pending`.
4. The request confirms with `?confirm=<fingerprint>`. The server takes
   the database's write lock through `database.take_write_lock` (#438),
   the one helper every write that holds the lock uses, as Delete All does
   for its check, copy and delete (`services/wipe.py:88`), and under it
   makes the copy (no copy, no save, as for Delete All, #256), then makes
   the write and its recalculation, which is the "with" pass itself, and
   works out the fingerprint again the same way the dry run did (against
   the stored figures when the without pass failed). It commits only if
   the fingerprint is the same; if
   the ledger changed meanwhile (the AI and the owner at once), it rolls
   back and answers with a new 409 (the copy is harmless). So a confirm
   always matches what the user saw, can't be sent blind, and costs one
   replay; two, under the lock, while `recalculated_by` doesn't match,
   since the without pass runs again.
   **No price is asked for under the lock** (#442, for #426; AGENTS.md,
   "Prices"): the confirmed write runs in `hourly_prices.with_prices_found`,
   as every save does since 1.2.9. Step 1 stored the write's own prices
   first, so the usual confirm asks for none; a price it still needs (one
   only a recalculation fills, such as an old row's missing value) rolls
   it back, the price is found with the lock free, and the write runs
   again from the request as given, copy and fingerprint included. A run
   rolled back that way removes its own copy, so one confirmed save
   leaves one copy (the "pre-change" and AI limits below count saves, not
   attempts). The copy:
   - with a login, `migrate.backup_sqlite(..., kind="pre-change",
     keep=5)`, a new kind added to `COPY_KINDS`, so it never pushes out
     the pre-upgrade copies. Like the AI's copies (`_ai_copies_held`,
     `migrate.py:258`), it also holds, for a week, the first copy of each
     day, so a run of confirmed saves can't push out the copy from before
     the first of them (#333's review). Not "before-move", as P2 had it: 1.x lists
     its "before" copies as every `btctx-before-*.db` but the kinds it
     knows (`migrate.py:216-229`, `:221`), so after going back to 1.x
     (5.16) such copies would count as 1.x's "before" copies and push out
     its older pre-upgrade ones, like the `btctx-before-schema-0010-….db`
     that #325's Ledger Review text sends users to (P3, 6);
   - with the AI key, a fresh AI copy at every confirmed write (its kind
     and limits, AGENTS.md "Security rules"), not only the hourly one
     `routers/transaction.py:129-130` makes before the first unconfirmed
     PUT, so the AI's writes can't push out the owner's copies.

The 409 reaches the AI too (the route is on `AI_KEY_ROUTES` already). The
guide (5.12) and `confirm_move`'s own description tell it to show the
user the years and confirm only after the user says yes. The form shows

with "Save anyway (a copy is made first)" and "Cancel". This covers
today's accounts too: moving a 1.x deposit from Wallet to Exchange BTC

**Catching up on a past year** (the lead's question on #307). Every

**What it costs (measured by P3, 6).** One replay of the whole ledger on

**A user who pooled wallets or exchanges in 1.x (Q5).** Nothing rewrites

### 5.16 Rollback

**A build step (#251, decision 2).** Before a step's pull request merges
into `2.0`, the tag `pin/2.0-step-N` marks `2.0`'s tip. A merged step that
goes wrong is undone by a pull request reverting its squashed commit, so
`2.0` is back at the pin's state, plus any `develop` merge made since
(#251, decision 3); `develop` is never rewound. A test database
that ran the step (the VM's, a temp copy; never the owner's) goes back by
its copy:

| Step (5.17) | Undoing it | A test database that ran it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 second calculation | revert | nothing to do |
| 2 data model, migration, book | revert | its pre-2.0 copy (5.3): once reverted, the app refuses a database at 2.0's revision, as 1.x does |
| 3 by currency, rules | revert | nothing (no schema change) |
| 4 forms, report | revert | nothing |
| 5 imports, export, connector | revert (the connector reaches PyPI only with 2.0's pre-releases, B10a, and its release; a pre-release's connector is fixed forward like the rest of it) | nothing |
| 6 form, Q4 | revert | nothing |
| 7 the gate | revert closes it: rows saved into new accounts stay and replay, but can't be edited | the copy made before its test |
| 8 screens | revert | nothing |
| 9 texts | revert | nothing |
| 10a pre-releases (7.4, B10a) | none once one is published: a pre-release is fixed forward, in the next pre-release (SECURITY.md), and its migrations' ids never change again (5.3) | testers go back as below; the Mac beta app never had their ledger (B10a) |
| 10b release | none: a released 2.0 is fixed forward, by a 2.0.x | users go back as below |

Steps 3 to 8 all use step 2's account book, so once one of them has
merged, step 2 can't be reverted by itself: undoing it means reverting
every later step first, newest first. Steps 6 and 7 revert together once
7 has merged: reverting 6 alone would put the old Buy and Sell form in
front of an open gate. The frontend lint against comparing an id with
1, 2, 3 or 4 (5.4) is switched on with the last screen that still has
such comparisons (step 8), not with step 2.

**Going back to 1.x (Q21),** for users. Written in `docs/MAINTENANCE.md`
(Docker, the Mac app) and in 2.0's release notes, not in
`startos/instructions.md`: Start9's guide makes the release notes "the

- **StartOS:** as Q21 says, uninstall BitcoinTX (which deletes its data),
  then restore the StartOS backup made before the update. The package
  doesn't downgrade (`down: IMPOSSIBLE`,
  `startos/startos/versions/current.ts:233`), and StartOS gives this way
  back in its own words: "To return to %{to}, uninstall the service —
  which deletes its data — and restore a backup made while it was
  running %{to}." (`shared-libs/crates/start-core/locales/i18n.yaml:2836`;
  Start9's guide, `versions.md`, "Migrations": "uninstall and restore a
  backup instead"). It works because a StartOS backup holds the installed
  package itself and a restore reinstalls that package with its own data
  version (StartOS's code, `start-technologies` at 9a6fc80:
  `shared-libs/crates/start-core/src/service/mod.rs:816-826` writes
  `<id>.s9pk` into the backup, `backup/restore.rs:225-233` installs it,
  `projects/start-sdk/lib/backup/Backups.ts:1084-1089` puts back the
  data version), so a backup made under 1.x comes back as 1.x; a restore
  over an installed package is refused (`service/service_map.rs:389-394`).
  A correction P2 made earlier (that a restore migrates forward, from
  `startos/README.md:169`'s wording) was wrong and is undone; that line,
  and `:177` and `instructions.md:83`, which leave out the uninstall, are
  #323.

    `manifest.md`, "Pre-download alerts"): 2.0's manifest sets one,
    shown before the download for any installed version below
    `2.0.0:0`, in all five languages: "**Make a StartOS backup of
    BitcoinTX** before updating, and download BitcoinTX's own backup
    (Settings, Backup & Restore): you need one of them to go back to
    1.x." The release notes repeat it with the full steps.
  - **Keep that backup.** A StartOS backup target holds one backup per
    package, and the next backup of BitcoinTX to it replaces the 1.x
    one, package included (`disk/mount/backup.rs:206-223`). Until the
    user is sure of 2.0, they back 2.0 up to another drive.
  - **If the StartOS backup is gone:** install 1.x's package from its

### 7.2 What every build session does

Every build issue's checklist from B1 on starts and ends this way, and
its kickoff prompt says so (B0 is 7.3):

1. **Start:** `make sync-check`; read #251 (the decisions and the index),
   the step's issue, the parts of section 5 it names, this section, and
   AGENTS.md. Never the owner's real ledger (the installed Mac app's
   data, `127.0.0.1:8765`, the `bitcointx` MCP tools): temp databases, the
   reference test ledger and the StartOS test VM only.
2. **Bring `develop` in first** (#251, decision 3). `make catch-up-2.0`
   (`scripts/catch-up-2.0.sh`, run before every step and nightly) does it
   when the merge is clean (no 1.x migration, no change to the reference
   test ledger or its figures, no conflict, no step with auto-merge on): a
   merge commit GitHub makes on a branch of its own, merged once its own CI
   passes, with no review of its own (the owner's decision 24,
   2026-10-23). Otherwise it stops, and the lead does the rest of this
   point by hand. If `develop` has
   commits `2.0` hasn't (`git log origin/2.0..origin/develop`), a pull
   request of its own, before any of the step's work:
   - a branch `2.0-merge-develop-<date>` from `origin/2.0`, then
     `git merge origin/develop`, never a rebase;
   - a 1.x migration that came in: until the first pre-release,
     `v2.0.0-alpha.1` (the owner's decision 28), is published (B10a, point 4: from then on anyone
     may have installed it), 2.0's migrations are put after it: 2.0's
     first migration's `down_revision` is re-pointed to 1.x's new head,
     and no file, `revision` or `STARTS_2_0` marker moves (2.0's ids
     start at 0100, 5.3); after that, the 1.x migration stays as it is
     and an Alembic merge revision joins it to 2.0's head (5.3). #251's
     index says which applies;
   - when it brought a 1.x migration or new reference-ledger rows,
     `reference_1x.db` and its figures are made again with B1's script;
   - done when `make check` passes, the reference figures and the
     independent check agree, and nothing of 2.0's is lost: every merged
     step's tests still pass, and `git diff origin/2.0 HEAD` shows only
     what `develop` brought, with any re-pointing;
   - its review is sized by what `develop` brought (AGENTS.md,
     "Reviews"); a re-pointed migration or a merge revision is the
     database, so it follows the figures rule: every figures check and
     the backup and restore tried on a 1.x ledger. Any figure it moves
     must be one a `develop` pull request moved on purpose, with its
     before-and-after OK'd there; any other moved figure stops it until
     it's fixed;
   - **once a pre-release has bumped `2.0`'s version** (B10a, point 3),
     a 1.x release on `develop` conflicts with it on the version files:
     `VERSION`, `desktop/BitcoinTX.spec`, the manifest's image tag,
     `mcp_server/pyproject.toml`, `startos/startos/versions/current.ts`
     and `versions/index.ts`, the release notes in the i18n texts, and
     the price history's `backend/assets/btc_prices/MANIFEST.csv` and
     year files. The merge keeps `2.0`'s version and release notes; if
     that 1.x `current.ts` carries an `up` that does work, its version is
     spun off on `2.0` too (`v1.2.N_0.ts` in `other`, Start9's
     `versions.md`), so an install of it updating straight to 2.0 keeps
     that migration; and the price history is made again with `make
     prices` (stored hours never change, so both sides' hours are kept);
   - takes no pin of its own: a pin marks a build step (#251, decision
     2), and the next step's is tagged after it;
   - reported "ready" like any pull request (point 5), and merged by the
     build's lead with a **merge commit**, never a squash:
     `gh pr merge N --merge --match-head-commit <sha>`. A squash
     would leave `develop`'s commits out of `2.0`'s history, and the next
     merge would bring them in again (Start9's guide says the same of any
     long-lived branch: "Merge a long-lived branch with a merge commit,
     never a squash", `maintaining-a-package.md`).

   If `develop` has nothing new, the issue says so and the step starts.
   When sessions start together (B1 with B2a; B4, B5a, B6a and B6p; B8
   with B5b; and B7b and B8 when they start building after B3; P6 and P7
   are planning cards, with no code and no `develop` merge), the first
   of them makes the one `develop` merge, and the others start from
   `2.0` once it has merged, so `2.0`'s migrations are never re-pointed
   twice at once.
3. **Branch:** `2.0-b<n>-<what>` from `origin/2.0`. Not `2.0/…`: git can't
   keep a branch `2.0/x` beside a branch `2.0`. The pull request:
   `gh pr create --base 2.0`, filled from
   `.github/pull_request_template.md`, titled "2.0-B<n>: …". A branch
   that falls behind `2.0` merges `origin/2.0` in, never a rebase.
4. **Prove 1.x unchanged** (5.15), besides the step's own tests:
   `test_seed_ledger.py` and `test_independent_figures.py` pass, every
   figure unchanged (B4's test takes the report's new keys only; in B7a
   only the new rows' years move); B1's upgraded-1.x test passes;
   `scripts/equivalence_check.py` says "No difference", or shows only
   the step's intended differences, each listed in the pull request:
   `--against origin/2.0` while working, and `--against
   pin/2.0-step-<n>` once point 5 has taken the pin; `make check`; CI
   green.
5. **Ready, the pin, and the lead's auto-merge** (AGENTS.md, "Big work":
   "The lead runs the merges", the owner's rule of 2026-10-17, #420; the
   lead turns on auto-merge instead of merging, since the staging flow,
   #591). A session never turns on auto-merge. Once the review (and its
   re-check, where "Reviews" asks one) has nothing open, it posts
   "ready: #N" on #251, where the build's lead keeps the queue, and tells
   the lead. There is no one-at-a-time update queue: "Protect 2.0" asks no
   branch to be up to date (7.3), as "Protect staging" doesn't. When the
   step's turn comes (the step before it has merged), the lead:
   - tags `2.0`'s tip, the pin, right before turning on auto-merge, while
     no other pull request into `2.0` has auto-merge on, so nothing else
     merges into `2.0` before this step:
     `git fetch origin && git tag pin/2.0-step-<n> origin/2.0 && git push
     origin pin/2.0-step-<n>`. If `2.0` moved since the session's point
     4, the session runs the equivalence check again against the pin and
     posts it first;
   - checks that the owner's "merge" (B9, B10a, B10b), or the owner's OK
     on a before-and-after (a figure moved on purpose), is quoted on the
     pull request, with its date, where one is needed; a step under the
     figures rule that moves no figure needs neither;
   - turns on auto-merge with a squash:
     `gh pr merge N --auto --squash --match-head-commit <sha>`. GitHub
     merges it once "CI passed" passes on the medium set, and the merge
     starts the full set on `2.0` (7.3, point 1). On a conflict or red
     checks, the step's session merges `origin/2.0` in and fixes what
     broke; a red `2.0` is fixed before anything else merges into it.

   A pin is never moved or deleted (7.3). Steps that run at once still
   merge one at a time: the next one gets auto-merge only after the one
   before has merged and its own pin is taken.
6. **Reviews** as AGENTS.md says ("Reviews"): a fresh read-only reviewer
   posted on the pull request; app code gets a re-check of the fixes;
   the steps under the figures rule and the owner's "merge" steps
   (7.1's last column) also show point 4's before-and-after comparison,
   and a database step its backup and restore tried on a 1.x ledger.
7. **CHANGELOG:** entries go under `## [Unreleased 2.0]`, a section of its
   own above `## [Unreleased]`, so 1.x's entries and 2.0's never share
   one. A `develop` merge that conflicts there keeps both, 2.0's section
   first. Each pre-release turns it into its own `## [v2.0.0-beta.N]`
   (B10a), and B10b folds them all into `## [v2.0.0]`.
8. **End:** the issue ticked, with a summary comment; the step's row in
   #251's index; the session's own lines on #41 (read just before
   editing); "ready: #N" on #251 and the build's lead told (point 5);
   `make sync-check`.

A new backend module gets its `desktop/BitcoinTX.spec` `hiddenimports`
entry (AGENTS.md, "Before you change…"). A write that must hold SQLite's
write lock takes it through `database.take_write_lock` (#438), and a new
way to save or recalculate runs in `hourly_prices.with_prices_found`, so
