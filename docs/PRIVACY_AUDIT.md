# Privacy and leak audit

## Every request BitcoinTX can make (1.2.8, re-checked for 1.2.9 and 2.0)

1.2.8's privacy audit, then planned as the stable version (#151; develop 72ba76f, 2026-10-12,
re-checked on 16bf055, 2026-10-13; for 1.2.9 on b785a00b, 2026-10-16,
and for 2.0 on 4567c082, 2026-10-29, below the table). It supersedes the 1.2.1 audit's table where they differ
(Coinbase and CoinGecko are gone, hourly prices are new); that audit
stays as the record of its fixes, in
`docs/archive/PRIVACY_AUDIT_1.2.1.md`. When a change
touches what leaves the machine, check it against this table and update
it.

**Result: nothing goes out before the owner chooses, no request names a date or an hour, Tor carries every request to a public site whenever it's set (except Bitstamp's fixed history blocks once the owner answers Yes to downloading them without Tor, #241: below), the download checks its fingerprints and never changes a stored price, and nothing goes to Coinbase or CoinGecko.** Each row is pinned by the tests named (all in `backend/tests/` unless said), and was also observed: Claude drove a real app (temp database) through the price paths under 13 choices with a recording fake internet (table at the end).

**What every request to a public site reveals:** the server's IP address (unless Tor, or a VPN such as StartOS's Set Outbound Gateway), the time (that BitcoinTX is open, or that a price was needed just then), and a generic client (`python-httpx`, the same for every install). Never a ledger date, hour, amount or address.

**Bitstamp's history without Tor (#241, the owner's decision of 2026-10-16; 1.2.9):** through Tor, Bitstamp refuses BitcoinTX's requests (a 403 or a "Loading" page), so rows 5 to 7's Bitstamp blocks never arrive. Once Bitstamp has refused through the proxy (never before: a Tor that isn't running is no refusal), the price card asks once, on the Dashboard and in Settings: "Download the price history once without Tor? It's the same download for every install, and names none of your dates; Bitstamp would see this server's address." The answer is recorded (when, and on the Dashboard or in Settings), login only (never the AI key), kept across restarts and by an in-app restore, and changeable in Settings, even while the server sets the choice (StartOS's action: its Use Tor text says so). **No** changes nothing. **Yes**, while a proxy is set and public sites are allowed (checked before each request), sends directly only rows 5 to 7's fixed blocks: `ohlc/btcusd/?step=3600|86400&limit=1000&start=<a block start>`; `outbound.FixedBlocksOnly` refuses anything else on that route before it's sent (another site, Bitstamp without `start`, a `start` that isn't one of those fixed block starts, which no ledger decides). Rows 2, 3 and 5's latest days stay on Tor. Bitstamp then sees this server's IP address and when: the year files once (at start or at the Yes: nothing in them depends on the ledger), the daily history once (when a value first needs a day that isn't stored), and the newer hours at most every 10 minutes when a value needs an hour after the year files (that a recent value is priced, never which hour): what it sees today without Tor. Tests: `test_history_without_tor.py` (every one), `frontend/e2e/price-card.e2e.ts` ("through a Tor Bitstamp refuses"). Observed on the lab VM over Start9's real Tor service: see #389.

**A change of the choice while a lookup is under way (#437; 1.2.9):** a lookup can outlast a save of Settings → Privacy & network (it may wait for your own Mempool for up to 90 seconds before turning to the public sites). Each request is checked against the settings in use just before it's sent: one to a public site goes only while they allow public sites, through the same proxy its client was made with (`outbound.async_client`, also under `history_client`), and one to your Mempool only to the address in use, with the same proxy and trusted root (`outbound.own_node_client`); any other is refused before it's sent (`outbound.ChoiceChanged`), and the daily history, the latest days, the hours after the year files (the blocks already answered are kept, #475) and your Mempool's history are asked again at the next lookup, as the new choice says. An .onion address typed with a dot at its end (`….onion.`) or with a full stop the address's IDNA makes a dot (`。`, `．`) is an .onion too, judged on the host httpx connects to (`outbound._is_onion`; the Settings form's `isOnion` on the browser's reading, which agrees): it needs the proxy; and an address whose host httpx would keep as typed (a backslash, a `%` escape) is refused (`outbound._ordinary_host`), and one saved before is never asked (`own_node_client`). Found by the review of #443; before, such an .onion was asked directly, even with Tor set. Before, a lookup that started under "your Mempool, with public sites as backup, through Tor" and outlasted a save of Nobody with no proxy asked Bitstamp directly. Tests: `test_choice_at_each_request.py` (every one), `test_hours_clock_reset_475.py`.

**A proxy set for the whole machine (#474, the owner's rule of 2026-10-17; 1.2.9):** `HTTPS_PROXY`, `HTTP_PROXY` or `ALL_PROXY` in BitcoinTX's environment, or a Mac's system proxy (System Settings › Network › Proxies). It carries only requests BitcoinTX sends without a proxy of its own to addresses beyond this computer and the home network: the public sites (rows 2, 3, 5 to 7; on Docker, a way to hide the server's address, and BitcoinTX's proxy setting or the server's Tor replaces it whenever set), your Mempool at an internet address (rows 1, 3 and 4: BitcoinTX's own proxy carries only an .onion, so this one follows a machine-wide proxy even with Tor set), and the IRS draft forms (row 9). It never carries a request to this computer or the home network: your Mempool at `localhost`, 127.x, `::1`, a `.local` or `.lan` name, a one-word name, 10.x, 172.16–31.x or 192.168.x (the home addresses "Trust this server" accepts, and this computer, each as the resolver reads it: `outbound.this_computer_or_home`); the Mac app's own checks of 127.0.0.1 at start (`desktop_ports.open_here`: no proxy at all); and the AI connector's requests to the Mac app (with its key file, every request) or to a `BTCTX_URL` at such an address (row 11; its own copy of the rule, `btctx_mcp.client.this_computer_or_home`). Nor does it carry the history without Tor after a Yes (rows 5 to 7's fixed blocks): those go directly, so Bitstamp sees this server's address, as the card says. So "direct" in this table means no proxy at all for this computer, the home network and the history without Tor, and no proxy of BitcoinTX's for an internet address. Only the route changed: certificates are checked as any client's (`SSL_CERT_FILE` included). Before, your Mempool at a home address and the history without Tor went through such a proxy (on a Mac, whatever its list of exceptions said), and so did the Mac app's checks under a system proxy whose exceptions didn't name 127.0.0.1, or `HTTP_PROXY` for an app started from a shell that set it (a proxy elsewhere can't reach this Mac's 127.0.0.1, so the app quit after 30 seconds). Someone who reached a home Mempool, or BitcoinTX from the AI connector, only through such a proxy (an SSH tunnel as `ALL_PROXY`) now can't: the owner's "never". StartOS's package sets none of these. Tests: `test_machine_proxy_474.py` (every one: each client asked which way httpx would send a request, with no proxy, the variables, and a Mac's system proxy with its default exceptions).

**Nothing before the owner chooses:** a fresh install has the source `unset` and asks nobody (`test_a_fresh_install_asks_nothing_until_the_owner_chooses`, `test_off_or_unchosen_asks_nobody`, `test_entries_made_before_choosing_never_turn_public_sites_on`); a stored choice with no record of when it was made sends nothing until the owner confirms it (`test_price_confirm.py`, the plan's 10 cases); invalid server settings, or an invalid server Tor, ask nobody (`test_invalid_server_settings_turn_lookups_off`, `test_a_bad_server_tor_asks_nobody_and_a_server_set_choice_ignores_it`); a command never makes a choice (#134, `test_10_a_command_and_the_ai_key_never_write_the_record`), and one run without the server's price settings the app started with (StartOS, by hand) asks nobody (#461, `test_cli_server_prices_461.py`). On StartOS the price step is critical at install, before the service starts (release test INS-2), and the app asks nothing while it's unset anyway.

**Outside BitcoinTX (for completeness):** StartOS's own screens show the Mempool and Tor dependency icons from GitHub (`startos/startos/dependencies.ts`, commit-pinned; Start9's dependency metadata, `dependencies.md`). Mempool's own exchange-rate requests are Mempool's (its own Tor setting; `instructions.md` says so). The AI app downloads the connector from PyPI (`uvx btctx-mcp==X.Y.Z`). The developer tools (`make prices`, the monthly and IRS watch workflows) run on GitHub or the developer's computer, never in an install.
